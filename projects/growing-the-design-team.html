Overview

I was Design Manager at Atlas, a team embedded at ArcTouch in Amsterdam. I managed 12 product designers while the whole design team was growing, and I worked with leadership on the business side of design: client pitches, estimates, audits, staffing and planning.

The problem

Three things had to improve at the same time as the team grew:

  • Growth was unstructured. Designers needed clear expectations for what each level looks like, and managers needed a fair way to defend promotions.
  • Hiring quality. The team was growing quickly, and every new hire had to be good at the craft and a good fit for the culture.
  • Scale. Onboarding, documentation and knowledge-sharing depended too much on individual people.

What I did

Career growth. I created structured career frameworks and mentoring programs, so each designer knew their gaps and what to work on next. I set clear expectations in three areas: visibility (use team channels, not only private messages), ownership (designers are often solo or in pairs on a project, so supporting peers and proposing improvements is part of the job) and documentation (design handoff documentation is required). Reviews and development plans moved into a shared tool, with 1:1s as a shared responsibility between manager and designer.

Hiring. I led portfolio reviews and interviews, and ran hiring calibration so the whole panel judged candidates the same way.

Scaling management. As the team grew, we did not want designers to lack management support. We added a third person to the management team, with a gradual transition over several months, and created a Master Designer role to raise the quality of the work by spreading good practices and following designers’ daily projects.

Knowledge sharing. I built onboarding, documentation and knowledge-sharing processes designed to be ready for AI tools, and I gave talks to the team, like the one on asynchronous communication in the case before this one. I also started tracking internal design requests in a dedicated project, so we could make decisions with data on how much effort they take.

The outcome

Four designers were promoted to Senior in one year, onboarding and documentation improved, and the team grew from 20 to 26 designers while keeping a strong culture fit. Design delivery also lined up better with commercial opportunities, because designers and managers were involved earlier in pitches, estimates and staffing.
