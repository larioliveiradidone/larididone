04 / LEADERSHIP

Leadership – Assync Communication Training

A talk for ArcTouch designers on how to write messages people can act on without a meeting.

Client
ArcTouch (internal)
Role
Design Manager
Skills
  • Facilitation
  • Communication
  • Team coaching

Overview

ArcTouch is client-oriented and has a hybrid structure, so designers split their energy between several projects, clients and internal initiatives. I gave a talk called “How to Be a Pro at Asynchronous Communication (Even If You Hate It)” to help the team adapt how they communicate.

The problem

Switching between projects, clients and initiatives all day is exhausting, and meetings are not always the best use of anyone’s time. The talk was not against synchronous communication, which is important. It made the case that both have a place:

  • Asynchronous (email, messages, documents) works well for team announcements, high-level planning discussions, one-directional feedback and documentation.
  • Synchronous (video calls, huddles, workshops) works well for collaborative meetings, team building, performance reviews and 1:1s.

What I did

I built the talk around two reasons to do more async: respect other people’s time, and make sure your message comes across. Then I gave the team practical tips for async messaging:

  • Be intentional.
  • Be clear and concise.
  • Make action items stand out with line breaks, bullet points and bold text.
  • Keep it casual, but not too casual.
  • Use threads to keep channels easy to follow.
  • Think before sending: tag the right people, check you are in the right channel, pick the right time (or schedule the message) and double-check the content and grammar.

To put it all together, I taught the Minto Pyramid: start with the answer, then support it. We walked through two examples, project updates and discovery results. The tips draw on nohello.net, BetterUp, ChartHop and Filippos Protogeridis.

The outcome

The team got a short, shared guide for writing messages that are easy to scan and act on, whatever the channel.
